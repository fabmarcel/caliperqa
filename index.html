Auditable verification of AI-generated work

Healthcare, finance, and insurance teams are shipping AI features into processes that get examined later. The question is not whether the model sounds right. It is whether you can show, months from now, how a given output was checked and what happened when it failed. I build that record.

  1. DiagnoseMap where AI output enters your process, what a wrong answer costs, and what evidence you are missing today.
  2. ExecuteBuild the evaluation set, run it against your feature, and document failure modes with reproducible cases.
  3. VerifyHand over a scored report, a regression suite your team can rerun, and the paper trail for whoever asks.

Who you are hiring

I am Fabián Arce. Nineteen years in enterprise systems, eleven of them in quality engineering. Before that I built ETL pipelines and reporting systems for Procter & Gamble, which is why reconciliation work comes naturally: I have been on both sides of the data, building the pipeline and then breaking it.

Most of my career has been spent on software that moves money. Four years owning quality end to end on a digital banking platform — accounts, authorization, interbank transfers, check processing, and the reconciliation between them. Then Salesforce CPQ and Logik.io at Teradyne: product configuration, pricing rules, approvals, bundles, discounts, and everything downstream to invoicing. Caliper QA is how I take that work directly to the teams that need it, without a layer of account managers in between.

Most of what I find lives at the edges. The order with one line item priced at zero. The renewal that crosses a fiscal year. The customer record with an apostrophe in the name. The AI response that is confidently wrong only when the input is empty. Happy paths get tested by everyone; the cases that reach production untested are the ones nobody thought to write down, and finding them is the part of this work I am best at.

I work from Heredia, Costa Rica, in US time zones, and I contract as an independent professional. The person on the call is the person who runs the tests and writes the report.
